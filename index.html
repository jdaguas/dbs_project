CivicPulse

Follow the bills that matter to you.

Information on federal legislation is public but scattered. CivicPulse gives voters and curious people one nonpartisan place to find bills on the issues they care about, see where those bills stand in Congress, and check back on them.

The problem

When people hear about a bill (on the news, from a friend, on social media), finding out what it actually does and what happened to it later takes work. The information is spread across Congress.gov, news sites, and lawmakers' own pages. Most people look once, then never check back.

Tools like Congress.gov and GovTrack already exist and are thorough. I'm not claiming the idea is new. CivicPulse is meant to be a simpler, more personal path: discover → understand → follow → check back, organized around topics a person cares about rather than bill numbers.

Hypothesis I'm testing: people would value a personal watchlist of bills, organized by their policy interests, with plain status updates and links back to the official sources.

Who it's for, and what they told me

Primary user: U.S. voters and civically curious people who want to keep up with federal legislation but don't regularly use Congress.gov.

Interviews

I asked each person: “Tell me about the last time you heard about a proposed law or bill you cared about. How did you learn what it would do, and did you ever check what happened afterward?”, followed by where they looked for updates, what was confusing, and what would have helped most.

How it works

  1. Pick your issuesHealthcare, education, housing, technology & AI, environment, and so on.
  2. Discover billsBrowse and search current federal bills, filtered by topic and recent activity.
  3. Understand a billOfficial title, summary (when one exists), current status, latest actions, and a timeline, all linked to the official record.
  4. See who's behind itOpen the sponsor's and cosponsors' profiles, with the roles shown clearly.
  5. Follow itAdd bills to a watchlist and come back to see what's changed.
  6. Go furtherRead related reporting (labeled separately from official records) and find official contact pages for your senators and representative.

Ground rules for accuracy

  • Strictly nonpartisan. No scores, ratings, or AI opinions on whether a bill is good or bad.
  • Precise terms. Sponsors introduce bills; the President signs or vetoes them. Sponsor and cosponsor roles are always labeled.
  • Stable IDs. Bills are tracked by Congress number + bill type + bill number (e.g., 119th Congress, H.R. 1234).
  • Sources first. Every status links to its primary record. News is labeled as third-party.
  • Honest gaps. Not every bill has an official summary or recorded vote. When something is missing, the page says so instead of filling it in.

My focus: Holistic Journey

CivicPulse's value is the whole path, from finding a bill to following it to coming back later. No single step is new, so the product only works if every step is connected and works on the live site. That fits Holistic Journey: one person's path from start to finish, touching design, systems, and users without going deep in any one of them.

I'm not choosing the AI focus on purpose. Having a model summarize or judge legislation conflicts with the nonpartisan, source-first goal, and getting it wrong would cost me users' trust.

Roadmap

Nov 3 – Nov 10

Prep

  • Get a Congress.gov API key and explore the bill, member, and action endpoints.
  • Check how reliable topic data (policy areas and subjects) is for filtering.
  • Finish user interviews and choose the main feature for following bills.
Nov 10 – Nov 17 · v1 due

v1: works end to end

  • Next.js app deployed on Vercel, with the Congress.gov API called server-side.
  • Bill browse and search, with topic filters only where the data supports them.
  • Bill detail page: official title, summary if available, status, latest actions, sponsor, and source links.
  • Basic member profile pages: sponsored vs. cosponsored bills, official website.
  • Sign-up and a simple watchlist, so real people can use it from start to finish.

Riskiest firstTopic filtering and API limits. If topic data is too sparse, v1 falls back to search plus recent activity.

Nov 18 – Nov 24

Put it in front of people

  • Get v1 into the hands of the people I interviewed, and watch where they get stuck.
  • Save topic preferences and build a personalized home feed.
  • Visual legislative timeline and progress indicator on bill pages.
Nov 25 – Dec 1 · Project fair

v2: polish and go further

  • “What changed” on the watchlist since your last visit.
  • Related news links with publication dates and clear source labels, kept only when they're about the exact bill.
  • Official contact links for your senators and representative, handling ZIP codes that span more than one district.
  • Stretch: email alerts when a followed bill's status changes.

Out of scope

  • State and local legislation, elections, and ballot matching.
  • Endorsements, political compatibility scores, or ratings of lawmakers.
  • AI-generated judgments about legislation.
  • Automated messages to representatives, mass emailing, or lobbying tools.
  • Real-time push notifications or complex data pipelines before basic tracking is reliable.

How I'll know it works

  • Each of my interviewees can find a bill they care about, understand its status, and follow it, without my help.
  • People come back to their watchlist after the first visit.
  • Every status shown matches the official Congress.gov record.

Tech stack, MCP servers & CLIs

The architecture follows the idea: official data in, cached on the server, a simple personal layer on top.

PieceChoiceWhy it fits
Frontend Next.js + Tailwind CSS Server-side data fetching keeps the API key private. Built-in caching means fewer API calls.
Data Congress.gov API (free key) The official source for bills, members, sponsors, cosponsors, summaries, and actions.
Accounts & data Supabase (Auth + Postgres) Sign-up, saved topics, and watchlists. Row-level security so each person only sees their own data.
Hosting Vercel (free tier) Deploys straight from GitHub, with preview URLs for testing with users.
News (v2) To be decided A news search API or hand-picked links, depending on free-tier terms and how relevant the results are.

MCP servers

  • Supabase MCP: lets Claude Code inspect tables, apply migrations, and check security advisors while I build the watchlist schema.

CLIs

  • Git / GitHub CLI: version control and the project repository.
  • Node.js / npm: running and building the Next.js app.
  • Vercel CLI: deploys, preview URLs, and keeping environment variables (the API key) out of the code.
  • Supabase CLI: a local database and migrations under version control.

Free tiers & limits

  • All services stay on the free tier, with no payment method added.
  • The Congress.gov API is rate-limited per key, so responses are cached on the server instead of being fetched on every page view.
  • Usage limits and alerts are turned on in Vercel and Supabase wherever those services offer them.
