CivicPulse

Know what Illinois actually passed, in plain English.

Illinois residents hear about new laws from the news but can't easily tell what those laws actually do. CivicPulse explains Illinois bills and laws in plain English, and every claim links back to the line of the bill it came from. Enter your address to see who represents you in Springfield and what they've sponsored.

The problem

Every January, local news runs a list of new Illinois laws taking effect. Between 300 and 400 took effect on January 1, 2026. Most people hear about them only from that list, from the news, or from politicians' promises, and the laws themselves are written in long, dense legal language.

I started this project because I wanted to know whether lawmakers were working on AI safeguards, and I realized I had never read a bill. I didn't know where to start, and the language put me off. The people I talked to had the same problem: they want to know what actually passed and what it means for them, without reading legal text.

Who it's for, and what they told me

Users: Illinois residents who follow the news but don't read legislation, and want to know what their state government has actually passed.

What I heard

I talked with three people, all in Illinois. These are paraphrased from my notes.

My mom · watches world news daily and political talk shows
She wants to see the tangible outputs of government: what lawmakers have actually accomplished, and how efficient they are.
My dad · follows politics in broad strokes
He doesn't care much about individual bills. He cares about what passed and how it affects him.
A friend in her 30s · concerned about Illinois specifically
She's curious about what passes in the state legislature, because she thinks the state has a lot of problems.
The moment: my parents are surprised every year when the local news announces the new laws going into effect on January 1.

What I learned

  • Everyone cared most about what passed. So the home page leads with laws taking effect and bills that have passed a chamber. Every pending bill is still searchable, because I also want to follow what's coming, like the AI bills I was curious about.
  • They hear about government secondhand, from news and talk shows, and want a way to check the record themselves.
  • State, not just federal. I originally planned a federal tracker, but Illinois laws are what showed up on my parents' local news, and what my friend asked about.

How it works

  1. See what's new, or search anythingThe home page shows laws about to take effect and bills that have passed at least one chamber. You can search every pending bill in the current session by keyword, sponsor, or bill number.
  2. Open a billGet a plain-English breakdown: its main point, what it actually requires versus what it only studies or reports on, and who it affects.
  3. Check the sourceEvery sentence in the breakdown sits next to the exact line of the bill it comes from, with a link to the official text.
  4. See who's behind itEach bill lists its House and Senate sponsors. Click a sponsor to see the other bills they've sponsored and how to contact them.
  5. Find your legislatorsEnter your Illinois address to find your state representative and state senator, with their official contact information and the bills they've sponsored. It uses your exact address, not just your ZIP code, because one ZIP code can span several districts.
  6. Follow itSign up, follow bills, and come back to see where they stand and when they take effect.

Ground rules

  • Nonpartisan. CivicPulse explains what a law says. It never says whether it's good or bad, and never scores lawmakers.
  • Sources first. Every claim is tied to the bill text. Sponsors and dates come straight from the official record.
  • Honest gaps. If the model can't support a claim with a quote, the claim isn't shown.

My focus: AI

I chose the AI focus because the people I talked to feel buried in news and big promises from politicians. They want to check what actually got passed, without having to read legal text. AI is what makes that possible: the app has a model read each bill and write the breakdown.

I want to get better at making AI responses trustworthy. For this project, that means accurate, sourced, and checkable. Two evals check every breakdown:

1. Quote check · catches made-up claims
The model must cite the exact bill text behind every claim. Code then checks that each quote really appears in the bill. Any claim with a quote that doesn't match fails.
2. Answer key · catches real quotes with the wrong meaning
I write correct breakdowns for 10 Illinois laws by hand, then score the model's breakdowns against mine, especially on “requires vs. studies,” which is easy to get wrong.

Roadmap

Nov 3 – Nov 10

Prep

  • Write the answer key: correct breakdowns of 10 Illinois laws, by hand.
  • Download sample bills from the Illinois General Assembly's file repository.
  • Get a first prompt working on one bill.
Nov 10 – Nov 17 · v1 due

v1: works end to end

  • Import every bill in the current session: status, sponsors, dates, and the official synopsis. No AI is needed for this part.
  • AI breakdowns with quoted sources, written the first time someone opens a bill and then saved. Bills that passed a chamber, and enacted laws, are explained in advance.
  • Bill page and a “new laws taking effect” list.
  • Sponsors on every bill page, linked to legislator pages.
  • Legislator lookup: enter an address to get your state representative and senator, with contact info.
  • Sign-up and following bills.
  • Quote check and answer-key evals running.
  • Deployed on Vercel.

Riskiest firstGetting accurate breakdowns of long legal text, which is why the answer key and the first prompt come before November 10.

Nov 18 – Nov 24

Test with real people

  • Give v1 to my mom, dad, and friend, and watch where they get stuck.
  • Improve the prompt based on which eval cases fail.
Nov 25 – Dec 1 · Project fair

v2

  • A “What changes for you on January 1, 2027” page, timed for the month before.
  • Filter by topic, such as AI and technology, housing, or schools.
  • Eval results shown on the site (“accurate on X of 10”).
  • “Your legislators' bills”: after the lookup, see what your own representative and senator sponsored, with plain-English breakdowns.
  • If time allows: related news links.

Out of scope

  • Federal legislation. My parents asked about Congress, so this is a future direction.
  • Matching laws to campaign promises, rating lawmakers, or predicting whether a bill will pass.
  • AI opinions on whether a law is good or bad.
  • Version-by-version diffs of bill text.
  • Email alerts (maybe after v2).
  • Sending messages to legislators from the app. CivicPulse shows official contact info; you reach out yourself.

How I'll know it works

  • My mom reads the new-laws list and can tell me, in her own words, what two of the laws do.
  • Breakdowns pass the quote check, and match my answer key on the 10 hand-written laws.

Tech stack, MCP servers & CLIs

PieceChoiceWhy it fits
Data Illinois General Assembly file repository Official, free, with no API key and nightly updates. It has every bill's status, actions with dates (including effective dates), sponsors, synopsis, and full text.
Address → district U.S. Census Geocoder Free, with no key. It turns an address into Illinois House and Senate districts, which is more accurate than matching by ZIP code.
Legislators Open States people data Free and openly licensed. It lists all 177 Illinois legislators by district, with email, phone, and office addresses. I'll download it rather than call the rate-limited API.
AI Claude API (Anthropic) Reads bill text and returns a structured breakdown with quotes. Each bill is explained only when someone first opens it, then saved, so I only pay for bills people actually read.
App Next.js + Tailwind CSS Runs model calls and data jobs on the server, so the API key stays private.
Database & accounts Supabase Sign-up, followed bills, and saved breakdowns. Row-level security keeps each person's follows private.
Hosting Vercel Deploys from GitHub. A scheduled job checks the Illinois repository for updates.

MCP servers

  • Supabase MCP: lets Claude Code inspect tables, apply migrations, and check security advisors while I build the schema.

CLIs

  • Git & GitHub CLI: version control and the repository.
  • Vercel CLI: deploys and environment variables (API keys stay out of the code).
  • Supabase CLI: a local database and migrations under version control.
  • Node.js / npm: running the app and the eval scripts.

Costs & limits

  • Vercel and Supabase stay on their free tiers.
  • The Claude API is the one paid piece. I'll set a monthly spend limit in the Anthropic Console before running anything.
  • Each bill version gets explained at most once. After that, every visitor sees the saved copy.
  • Only signed-in users can trigger a new breakdown, with a daily limit per person, so a bot clicking through thousands of bills can't drain the spending cap.
